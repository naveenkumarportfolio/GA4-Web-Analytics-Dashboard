# Web Analytics Dashboard & Digital Marketing Performance Analysis

### Google Analytics 4 | Google Merchandise Store Demo Account

## About the Project

I created this project as part of my Digital Marketing Analytics learning to understand how businesses use website data to evaluate their digital marketing performance.

For this project, I worked with the Google Merchandise Store Demo Account available in Google Analytics 4 (GA4). I studied different GA4 reports, collected the relevant metrics and organised them into an Excel dashboard.

My main objective was to understand where website visitors come from, how they interact with the website, which marketing channels generate revenue and where improvements can be made.

This project helped me connect marketing analytics concepts with practical business decision-making.

## Project Objectives

- Understand website traffic and visitor behaviour using GA4.
- Analyse new and returning users.
- Compare the performance of different acquisition channels.
- Measure website engagement and user interactions.
- Study important events and purchase-related metrics.
- Evaluate page-level performance.
- Identify marketing performance issues and opportunities.
- Build an Excel dashboard to present the findings clearly.

## Key Findings

### 1. Organic Search Performance

Organic Search generated approximately 25% of total sessions and 34.6% of total revenue.

It also recorded a 70.63% engagement rate, making it one of the stronger major acquisition channels.

### 2. Paid Search Performance

Paid Search contributed approximately 8.3% of sessions but only 2.9% of revenue.

Its revenue per session was approximately $0.74, compared with the overall average of $2.09.

This suggests that the quality of paid traffic needs further investigation.

### 3. Referral and Social Traffic

Referral and Organic Social generated approximately $6.23 and $7.21 per session respectively.

Although these channels contributed a small proportion of total traffic, their revenue per session was considerably higher than the overall average.

### 4. Website Engagement

The homepage received approximately 338,074 views, representing 25.2% of total page views.

However, its average engagement time was only 9 seconds.

This suggests an opportunity to improve homepage navigation and encourage visitors to explore product categories.

### 5. Customer Retention

Approximately 92.2% of users were new users.

This indicates an opportunity to improve customer retention through email marketing, remarketing and better post-purchase communication.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I identified the following areas for improvement:

1. Improve UTM tagging and GA4 traffic source tracking.
2. Focus on improving organic search performance.
3. Review paid search traffic quality before increasing advertising budgets.
4. Explore referral partnerships and social media campaigns.
5. Improve homepage navigation and product discovery.
6. Analyse the customer journey from product views to completed purchases.
7. Improve customer retention through email marketing.
8. Validate unusual event activity and conversion tracking before making business decisions.

## Important Data Observations

During the analysis, I also identified a few measurement issues that require further investigation:

- A sharp increase in product-list events around 28 September 2026.
- Checkout-page revenue that accounts for approximately 97.5% of reported revenue.
- Zero advertising cost, clicks and impressions in the campaign report.
- A large proportion of sessions classified as Direct or Unassigned.

These observations are treated as data-quality questions rather than confirmed marketing performance outcomes.

## Methodology

I used the Google Analytics 4 demo account reports as the source of information.

The relevant figures were transcribed into separate Excel worksheets. I then used Excel formulas to calculate additional performance indicators, including:

- Share of Sessions
- Share of Revenue
- Revenue per Session
- Revenue Index
- Engagement Rate

The dashboard is linked to the supporting worksheets so that the displayed results can be traced back to their source figures.

## Limitations

- The project uses Google Merchandise Store demonstration data, not private company data.
- The reporting window appears to cover August to October 2026; exact start and end dates were not captured.
- Some GA4 report totals differ slightly because of report definitions and attribution.
- Campaign advertising cost data was unavailable in the supplied extract.
- Purchase-related metrics should be interpreted carefully because the available purchase breakdown is incomplete.
- The project is based on a report extract rather than a direct GA4 API connection or automated live dashboard.
